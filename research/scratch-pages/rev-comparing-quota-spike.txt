**Key Point:**

The [Quota](/apigee/docs/api-platform/reference/policies/quota-policy), [SpikeArrest](/apigee/docs/api-platform/reference/policies/spike-arrest-policy), [PromptTokenLimit](/apigee/docs/api-platform/reference/policies/prompt-token-limit-policy), and [LLMTokenQuota](/apigee/docs/api-platform/reference/policies/llm-token-quota-policy) policies are all used for rate limiting, but they serve different purposes and operate on different metrics. While Quota and SpikeArrest focus on request counts, the PromptTokenLimit and LLMTokenQuota policies manage and control consumption based on tokens, which is crucial for AI and Large Language Model (LLM) workloads.

While SpikeArrest and PromptTokenLimit maintain counts with high reliability, they are designed to use a <a href="https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/intro/arch_overview/other_protocols/redis" class="external">Redis best-effort cache</a> to store their counts. Because the cache is not replicated, there are cases where counts may be lost, such as a restart of the cache servers, or other rare cases.

For these reasons, we recommend against using SpikeArrest or PromptTokenLimit for use cases that require accurate counting. Only the synchronous Quota or LLMTokenQuota policy offers accurate counting across all regions in a given timeframe.

**Tip:** You can also use the following to protect slow/sluggish backends without impacting the performance of the APIs:

- [Creating reusable shared flows:](/apigee/docs/api-platform/fundamentals/shared-flows) Combine policies and resources into a shared flow that you can consume from multiple API proxies, and even from other shared flows.
- [Chaining API proxies together](/apigee/docs/api-platform/fundamentals/connecting-proxies-other-proxies): Specify that one proxy is the target endpoint of another, effectively connecting the two proxies in a proxy chain. Chaining proxies in this way can help you avoid a network hop, and so improve overall performance.
